    What a strong clinician does here

    The trap in this case is anchoring — back pain in a 58-year-old reads as mechanical, so it's tempting to skip the history and start treating. The skill on display isn't spotting cancer. It's noticing a cluster: pain that's constant and worsening, doesn't ease with rest, wakes her at night, plus unintentional weight loss and a personal cancer history. No single item is alarming. Together they're a pattern that doesn't fit mechanical pain.

    The right move costs almost nothing: ask a few more questions, then send her to her physician with a clear note about what you found. Whether you're a PT, PTA, or massage therapist, the screen-and-refer decision is the same — recognizing who needs a medical workup is core to every license, and referring early is what changes outcomes.
